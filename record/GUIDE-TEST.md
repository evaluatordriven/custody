# How the reviewer's guide was tested (v0.9.3)

The guide was written for people who review work rather than build it. To test it, a careful reviewer
without a technical background read it with one sample record and answered three questions in her own
words. **The test measured the guide, not the reader.** Her answers were scored against a rubric fixed
before she answered.

## What the guide got right
- She found the language "a lot easier to understand" than the specification.
- Her decision rested on what the record actually said: she would not sign off, because one part of the
  work had not been checked.
- She correctly read what the work was measured against, who checked it, and what was found.

## Where the guide fell short (the test did not pass)
- The record did not make clear that the work was made by a tool. (The record has no field for this, so
  the guide and the plain view could not say it either.)
- The guide did not make clear that the work had **already been accepted** even though one part was not
  observed, which is the moment the standard is built around.

## What the reviewer found in the standard itself
- **A record shows only that the bar was frozen before the work was finished, not before it started.**
  The standard, the website and the guide all say "before the work starts". This is now the lead item for
  the next version.
- A reviewer first asks what a document is for and who it is for. Neither the record nor the guide's
  sign-off questions cover purpose and audience.
- The guide's links did not work once it was converted to a Word document.

Each of these is carried to v0.9.4. The scored answers are kept, and can be produced on request.
